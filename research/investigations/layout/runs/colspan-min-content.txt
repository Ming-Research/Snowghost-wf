Min-content width a column takes from a spanning cell (Chromium, headless, 1280x720)

Question: how a cell that spans several columns raises the columns' min-content widths when
its own min-content width exceeds their sum (the case that decides the column split of an
infobox whose image cell spans both columns).

Probe: tables of `width:min-content; border-spacing:0` in a 10px monospace font (a character
is 6.02 px), a first row with one cell spanning every column and holding a 300 px block, and a
second row with one cell per column: A "aaaa aaaa aaaa" (min 4 characters, max 14), B
"bbbbbbbb" (min = max = 8); these tables are among the cases of tests/layout/table-cases.html.
Widths of A and B in Chromium and under CSS Tables 3's proportional rule:

  A min..max (chars)  B min..max  spanning cell  Chromium A, B    CSS Tables 3 (weights: max)
  4..14               8..8        300 px         190.89, 109.11   169.00, 131.00
  4..39               8..8        300 px         248.92, 51.08    213.05, 86.95
  4..4                4..34       300 px         31.58, 268.42    50.61, 249.39
  22..22              4..34       300 px         132.45, 167.55   188.80, 111.20

Rule that fits all four: first each column grows from its min-content to its max-content width
in proportion to max - min (a column whose min equals its max takes nothing), until the spanning
cell's width is reached; what remains beyond the sum of the max-content widths is shared in
proportion to the max-content widths. Row 1: A reaches 84.3, B stays 48.2, the remaining
167.5 splits 0.636 : 0.364 (the max-content widths), giving 190.89 and 109.11. Row 4: A is already
at its max (132.45), B takes the rest (167.55) from its range. Three-column and further variants
agree. The max-content widths a spanning cell needs are shared as CSS Tables 3 gives them
(the tables of `width:max-content` among those cases agree).

Inline items: three deviations from Chromium 141 found in the 2026-10-02 full layout comparison, the rule each change
takes from the reference, and what tests/layout/inline-items-cases.html must show. The rules below are read from
Blink's LayoutNG inline layout (InlineItemsBuilder, LineBreaker, ShapeResult, InlineLayoutAlgorithm), not yet observed
here; the predictions are written before any run (body 16px/20px serif, a Times-metric face: space 4 px, "aaa"
21.3125 px and "bbb" 24 px in runs/float-intrinsic.txt).

1. Static position of an out-of-flow box inside a line.
Reference: InlineItemsBuilder keeps an out-of-flow box (and a float) as an opaque item of the inline content where it
occurs; InlineLayoutAlgorithm::PlaceOutOfFlowObjects gives an inline-level one with auto insets the inline offset its
item has on the line and the line box's top, and the line box is placed after text-align. A block-level one (its
display before blockification) goes to the start of the next line when inline content precedes it.
Snowghost put every out-of-flow box at the start of its paragraph (apollo11: two boxes, one a .sr-only span at x 263
where Chromium has 395.5). Now the walk anchors a box met after the paragraph's first piece in the paragraph (Mark's
Anchor), line layout records where the anchor falls (after text-align; after a forced break it belongs to the next
line; past hanging spaces it sits where they start) and the stacking pass reads it. The display before blockification
is not kept by the style stage, so a block-level box is placed as an inline-level one (board item
sg-abspos-original-display).
Cases, first section: each span.abs must sit, relative to its p, at the width of the text before it on its line and
at that line's top: "Before " on line 1; the second line of a wrapped paragraph; after a <br>, on line 2; in a centred
line, past the centring offset; inside a <b>. Before the change every span.abs sat at x 0, y 0 of its p.
Cases, fourth section (300 px wide): an anchor after the paragraph's trailing collapsible space. The reference's
InlineItemsBuilder treats out-of-flow and float items as opaque to white-space collapsing and removes the trailing
space at the block's end (CSS Text 3, 4.1.2: spaces at the end of a line are removed); line building keeps the space
in the text but ends the line's visible content and its text fragments before it and places an anchor past it where
it starts. Predicted:
  "text ", position: absolute span "x"   span at x = width of "text" (about 24, not 28), y 0 of its p, 8 x 20;
                                          the text node "text " 24 wide
  "text ", float: left span "x"          float at x 0, y 0 of its p, 8 x 20; "text " 24 wide, starting at x 8
The section adds five block-level boxes and four text nodes, all expected to match (floors 32 and 42).
New cases a-d, pending a CI result (floors not yet set), each with an anchor: (a) on a soft-wrapped line under
text-align: justify, after a justified gap; (b) right after preserved spaces hanging at a soft wrap under
white-space: pre-wrap; (c) right after a tab in white-space: pre; (d) in an inline-block whose paragraph ends with
preserved spaces under pre-wrap, testing the shrink-to-fit width. They add 9 block-level boxes, 1 inline-level box
and 12 text nodes.

2. Floats in a max-content width.
Reference: the max-content computation of an inline formatting context (InlineNode::ComputeMinMaxSizes) adds each
float's max-content margin box to the line between forced breaks the float's item sits in, and floats placed before
the content start the first line; runs/float-intrinsic.txt observed "float, aaa, br, bbb" at 51.3125 (30 + 21.3125),
where Snowghost gave 54 (30 + the widest line, 24).
Cases, second section (div.mc, display: flow-root and width: max-content, 2 px of border included in each width below;
flow-root because the stage applies a width keyword only to a box that establishes a formatting context, while a
plain block box takes its keyword as auto: the first CI run, without flow-root, gave each div.mc the full 1254 px of
its section where Chromium gives 53.3125, 98, 87.21875 and 98, a separate deviation this change does not address (board item sg-bl-02-72)):
  float, "aaa", br, "bbb"                       53.3125   (30 + 21.3125)
  float, "aaa", br, "bbbbbbbbbbbb"              98        (12 x 8; the float joins only "aaa")
  "aaa", br, float, "bb", br, "cccccccccccc"   87.21875  (the twelve c, wider than "bb" with the float, 46)
  "a", float, "a", br, "bbbbbbbbbbbb"           98        (the float joins the first line)
Before the change each added 30 to the widest line. The float in the third case still sits at its paragraph's top,
where Chromium puts it on the line of its anchor (y 20 in its div): Snowghost places a float met inside a paragraph
before the paragraph's lines, a separate deviation this change does not address (board item sg-float-in-paragraph-line; board item sg-bl-02-79 is a
different deviation, a float before a block inside an inline box), so that one float is the case page's
one expected block-level mismatch (floor 32 of 33).

3. Whether a line fits at the edge.
Reference: LineBreaker measures each item on a line in LayoutUnit: a text item's part on the line as
ShapeResult::SnappedWidth (its float width rounded up to 1/64 px), an inline box's start and end margin, border and
padding and an atomic inline's margin box exactly; items end at inline box edges, atomic inlines, out-of-flow and
float items, control items (tab, forced break) and text node boundaries, and also at script and bidi runs, which
Snowghost does not mark. Content fits while its sum is at most the available width plus LayoutUnit::Epsilon()
(AvailableWidthToFit, 1/64 px). Snowghost rounded the float sum of the whole line up once and allowed no epsilon, so
lines within 0.2 px of the edge broke differently (ecma262: 70 block-level boxes moved by up to 3.5 px). The
max-content width now sums the same rounded items, so content laid out in its own max-content width still fits.
Cases, third section (each div's height tells its line count, 20 px a line):
  100 px: inline-block 50, space, inline-block 46          1 line   (exact fit)
  100 px: inline-block 50, space, inline-block 46.015625   1 line   (1/64 over: the epsilon; Snowghost gave 2)
  100 px: inline-block 50, space, inline-block 46.03125    2 lines  (2/64 over)
  53.734375 px: six one-letter spans "a", then " a"         2 lines  (seven items, each rounded up, are 2 units
                                                                       over the width plus the epsilon; Snowghost,
                                                                       rounding once, gave 1)
  53.734375 px: "aaaaaa a" in one text node                 1 line   (one item)
The letter cases assume "a" is 454.5 or 454.67 layout units wide, as the Times-metric face and "aaa" = 21.3125
suggest; with another width they still pass, but may not have failed before.
Confidence: the epsilon and the per-item rounding of text widths, high (both are long-standing in LineBreaker); the
exact set of item edges, medium (script runs are not marked).

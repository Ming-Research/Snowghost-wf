Inline items: three deviations from Chromium 141 found in the 2026-10-02 full layout comparison, the rule each change
takes from the reference, and what tests/layout/inline-items-cases.html must show. The rules below are read from
Blink's LayoutNG inline layout (InlineItemsBuilder, LineBreaker, ShapeResult, InlineLayoutAlgorithm), not yet observed
here; the predictions are written before any run (body 16px/20px serif, a Times-metric face: space 4 px, "aaa"
21.3125 px and "bbb" 24 px in runs/float-intrinsic.txt).

1. Static position of an out-of-flow box inside a line.
Reference: InlineItemsBuilder keeps an out-of-flow box (and a float) as an opaque item of the inline content where it
occurs; InlineLayoutAlgorithm::PlaceOutOfFlowObjects gives an inline-level one with auto insets the inline offset its
item has on the line and the line box's top, and the line box is placed after text-align. A block-level one (its
display before blockification) goes to the start of the next line when inline content precedes it.
Snowghost put every out-of-flow box at the start of its paragraph (apollo11: two boxes, one a .sr-only span at x 263
where Chromium has 395.5). Now the walk anchors a box met after the paragraph's first piece in the paragraph (Mark's
Anchor), line layout records where the anchor falls (after text-align; after a forced break it belongs to the next
line; past hanging spaces it sits where they start) and the stacking pass reads it. The display before blockification
is not kept by the style stage, so a block-level box is placed as an inline-level one (board item
sg-abspos-original-display).
Cases, first section: each span.abs must sit, relative to its p, at the width of the text before it on its line and
at that line's top: "Before " on line 1; the second line of a wrapped paragraph; after a <br>, on line 2; in a centred
line, past the centring offset; inside a <b>. Before the change every span.abs sat at x 0, y 0 of its p.
Cases, fourth section (300 px wide): an anchor after the paragraph's trailing collapsible space. The reference's
InlineItemsBuilder treats out-of-flow and float items as opaque to white-space collapsing and removes the trailing
space at the block's end (CSS Text 3, 4.1.2: spaces at the end of a line are removed); line building keeps the space
in the text but ends the line's visible content and its text fragments before it and places an anchor past it where
it starts. Predicted:
  "text ", position: absolute span "x"   span at x = width of "text" (about 24, not 28), y 0 of its p, 8 x 20;
                                          the text node "text " 24 wide
  "text ", float: left span "x"          float at x 0, y 0 of its p, 8 x 20; "text " 24 wide, starting at x 8
The section adds five block-level boxes and four text nodes, all expected to match (floors 32 and 42).
New cases a-d, each with an anchor: (a) on a soft-wrapped line under
text-align: justify, after a justified gap; (b) right after preserved spaces hanging at a soft wrap under
white-space: pre-wrap; (c) right after a tab in white-space: pre; (d) in an inline-block whose paragraph ends with
preserved spaces under pre-wrap, testing the shrink-to-fit width. They add 9 block-level boxes, 1 inline-level box
and 12 text nodes.
Observed in Chromium 141 (CI runs 38329120282, 38334862078): case (b) puts the anchor at x 0, y 20 of its
paragraph, the start of the next line, not after the hanging spaces. Companion case (e), white-space: normal, eleven
"aaa " words and "a " (289.5 px with the space, 285.5 without), then the anchor and "tail" (about 20.5 px, which does
not fit), puts it at x 285.453125, y 0: on line 1, where the collapsible space starts. Case (f), the same text under
white-space: pre-wrap, keeps the anchor on line 1 after the space, and the text before it is one fragment, the space
included, not a fragment and a tail of spaces. The rule, from line_breaker.cc: IsTrailableItemType excludes
kOutOfFlowPositioned and BreakLine returns before a non-trailable item once the state is kTrailing, which pre-wrap
spaces that overflow the line reach (has_only_pre_wrap_trailing_spaces), so the anchor goes to the next line and the
spaces trail. When the spaces do not overflow (collapsible, or preserved and fitting), the line is not trailing,
HandleOutOfFlowPositioned gives the anchor the can_break_after of the text before it (LineBreakOofNoOrc),
HandleOverflow takes that break opportunity and RewindOverflow(i + 1) keeps the anchor on line 1, so the spaces are
not at the line's end. With no intervening inline box start, an anchor at a soft wrap's end with more content following
therefore belongs to the next line only when the line ends in preserved spaces whose full per-item-rounded width,
hanging past the end, overflows it, and always after a forced break. An intervening Open changes that rule, as the
completion-review findings below explain. The first prediction for (e), x 0, y 20 (a rule for preserved and collapsible spaces alike), was
refuted; an overflow test on the line's content width alone (which leaves hanging spaces out) failed (b).


2. Floats in a max-content width.
Reference: the max-content computation of an inline formatting context (InlineNode::ComputeMinMaxSizes) adds each
float's max-content margin box to the line between forced breaks the float's item sits in, and floats placed before
the content start the first line; runs/float-intrinsic.txt observed "float, aaa, br, bbb" at 51.3125 (30 + 21.3125),
where Snowghost gave 54 (30 + the widest line, 24).
Cases, second section (div.mc, display: flow-root and width: max-content, 2 px of border included in each width below;
flow-root because the stage applies a width keyword only to a box that establishes a formatting context, while a
plain block box takes its keyword as auto: the first CI run, without flow-root, gave each div.mc the full 1254 px of
its section where Chromium gives 53.3125, 98, 87.21875 and 98, a separate deviation this change does not address (board item sg-bl-02-72)):
  float, "aaa", br, "bbb"                       53.3125   (30 + 21.3125)
  float, "aaa", br, "bbbbbbbbbbbb"              98        (12 x 8; the float joins only "aaa")
  "aaa", br, float, "bb", br, "cccccccccccc"   87.21875  (the twelve c, wider than "bb" with the float, 46)
  "a", float, "a", br, "bbbbbbbbbbbb"           98        (the float joins the first line)
Before the change each added 30 to the widest line. The float in the third case still sits at its paragraph's top,
where Chromium puts it on the line of its anchor (y 20 in its div): Snowghost places a float met inside a paragraph
before the paragraph's lines, a separate deviation this change does not address (board item sg-float-in-paragraph-line; board item sg-bl-02-79 is a
different deviation, a float before a block inside an inline box), so that one float is the case page's
one expected block-level mismatch. With cases a-f the page's floors are 45 of 46 block-level boxes (that float the
only miss), 20 of 20 inline-level boxes and 60 of 60 text nodes (CI run 38346038904).

3. Whether a line fits at the edge.
Reference: LineBreaker measures each item on a line in LayoutUnit: a text item's part on the line as
ShapeResult::SnappedWidth (its float width rounded up to 1/64 px), an inline box's start and end margin, border and
padding and an atomic inline's margin box exactly; items end at inline box edges, atomic inlines, out-of-flow and
float items, control items (tab, forced break) and text node boundaries, and also at script and bidi runs, which
Snowghost does not mark. Content fits while its sum is at most the available width plus LayoutUnit::Epsilon()
(AvailableWidthToFit, 1/64 px). Snowghost rounded the float sum of the whole line up once and allowed no epsilon, so
lines within 0.2 px of the edge broke differently (ecma262: 70 block-level boxes moved by up to 3.5 px). The
max-content width now sums the same rounded items, so content laid out in its own max-content width still fits.
Cases, third section (each div's height tells its line count, 20 px a line):
  100 px: inline-block 50, space, inline-block 46          1 line   (exact fit)
  100 px: inline-block 50, space, inline-block 46.015625   1 line   (1/64 over: the epsilon; Snowghost gave 2)
  100 px: inline-block 50, space, inline-block 46.03125    2 lines  (2/64 over)
  53.734375 px: six one-letter spans "a", then " a"         2 lines  (seven items, each rounded up, are 2 units
                                                                       over the width plus the epsilon; Snowghost,
                                                                       rounding once, gave 1)
  53.734375 px: "aaaaaa a" in one text node                 1 line   (one item)
The letter cases assume "a" is 454.5 or 454.67 layout units wide, as the Times-metric face and "aaa" = 21.3125
suggest; with another width they still pass, but may not have failed before.
Confidence: the epsilon and the per-item rounding of text widths, high (both are long-standing in LineBreaker); the
exact set of item edges, medium (script runs are not marked).

Completion-review findings at 30ef599 (static source comparison; predictions before CI).
Reference for the line-breaker rules below:
https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/141.0.7390.37/third_party/blink/renderer/core/layout/inline/line_breaker.cc
Reference for intrinsic float contributions:
https://github.com/chromium/chromium/blob/141.0.7390.37/third_party/blink/renderer/core/layout/inline/inline_node.cc

F1. Confirmed: the preserved-space overflow test rounded the whole content once and the whole hanging tail once,
although next_line rounds each item before summing. LineBreaker uses ShapeResult::SnappedWidth for each text item
(line_breaker.cc 1437-1448, 1655) and adds item_result->inline_size to position_ (1361); its preserved trailing-space
overflow enters kTrailing (1398-1400), excluding a following out-of-flow item. The full line width must therefore
use the same per-item sum as next_line, including preserved spaces and tabs measured at their actual tab stops.
The line keeps that already computed full width, including the chosen break candidate's measure. This is chosen
over measuring the line again: retaining the sum adds no dependency, whereas another pass repeats the line's
left-to-right tab-position chain. The visible content width keeps its existing meaning for placement and alignment.
Prediction: where the once-rounded content plus once-rounded tail fits within available width plus 1/64 px but
the per-item total exceeds it, the anchor goes to the next line and the preserved spaces form a trailing fragment.
No new F1 case: the page's known "aaa" width is already snapped; it does not establish the raw one-letter width
needed for a reliable rounding-boundary case. The earlier six-span case explicitly assumes that geometry. No font
measurement is permitted in this worktree, so this prediction remains unverified rather than adding a guessed case.

F2. Confirmed: a float at the text's end was added before recognizing the mandatory break at that same position.
InlineNode measures each line separately (inline_node.cc 2098-2103), collects floats from that line's results
(2106-2139) and compares that line's total against the maximum (2154). HandleForcedLineBreak ends the line before
the next item (line_breaker.cc 2962). The terminal position now follows the same break-before-float rule as
an interior position. The shaped break array has no terminal cell, so the last forced-break scalar supplies that
boundary's judgment. New max-content case: div.mc containing "aaa", br, span.fl30, with no text after the float.
Prediction: its content width is max(width("aaa"), 30) = 30 px, and its bordered width is 32 px, rather than
53.3125 px. The float's vertical placement is still the known paragraph-float deviation; this fix concerns intrinsic
width only. This case adds 2 block-level boxes, 1 inline-level box (br), and 1 rendered text node.

F3. The scan stops at the Open as reported, but the claim that the wrapped anchor should stay on line 1 is refuted.
HandleOpenTag leaves can_break_after false (line_breaker.cc 4024). HandleOutOfFlowPositioned explicitly documents
"Text+SP OP OOF Text => !break_after" (3939) and copies the previous item's can_break_after (3945). RewindOverflow
remembers the unclosed Open (4395-4406) and rewinds before it upon encountering the non-trailable out-of-flow item
(4415-4418). Open and Anchor therefore move together in source order. The scan keeps its break. Its lookahead now
stops at the same deferred Open or Atomic, so an anchor behind one cannot falsely make preserved spaces non-trailing.
New case (e) companion: the existing eleven "aaa " words and "a ", followed by a plain span containing span.abs
"x" and "tail". Prediction: the anchor is at x 0, y 20 of its p, with the wrapper and "tail" on line 2; the existing
unwrapped case keeps its anchor on line 1. A second companion uses pre-wrap: the same next-line anchor, with the
fitting final space on line 1 reported as a separate trailing fragment. That companion detects the lookahead error;
the normal-space companion checks that Open and Anchor stay together. Each adds 2 block-level boxes, 1 inline-level
box (the wrapper), and 3 rendered text nodes. These predictions rely on LineBreakOofNoOrc, stable in Chromium 141
(runtime_enabled_features.json5 2932-2936), as did the earlier unwrapped observations.

Together the three new cases add 6 block-level boxes, 3 inline-level boxes and 7 rendered text nodes. The existing
page totals were 46, 20 and 60; predicted new totals are 52, 23 and 67. Whitespace-only source nodes between cases
are not rendered and do not enter these oracle totals. No floors in run.sh change. No local build, test or oracle
run was made; the new outcomes and totals await the primary agent's Chromium 141 comparison in CI.

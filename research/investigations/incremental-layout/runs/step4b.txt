Verdict
  Criteria 3 and 4 hold for style edits. Criterion 2 holds sequentially on
  all three pages and at four workers on ecma262 and html5, and fails at
  four workers on apollo11 (1.55), where the full layout takes about 30 ms
  and a root font change redoes nearly every paragraph; not yet attributed.
  An update that does no layout work costs 5 to 9 times as much at four
  workers as sequentially (colour edits: 27.4 against 3.8 ms on html5), the
  tree-wide validation and marking walk of styles_changed. Both are in
  docs/todo.md.

5. Clock readings and the earlier four-worker numbers
  The first par timing of this step read the clock with std::time::now in
  statements that neither read nor write what the timed work writes. Under
  [PAR-1] an overlapping lowering may run such adjacent statements
  together, so with --par a reading could be taken before the work it ends
  had finished; the sequential lowering overlaps nothing. The first par
  table reported html5 rootfont 1.37 and apollo11 2.43, with the full
  comparator at 238 to 250 ms on html5, about its build_boxes and
  prepare_text without lay_out. A minimal Whitefoot program written to
  reduce the gap (build/x5/wf-repro, not committed) showed no gap once its
  readings depended on its work, and showed readings taken before the work
  when they did not. The oracle now ends each interval with a reading that
  reads the layout the work writes (stamp) and starts the work with a call
  that takes the starting reading as an argument. On the same four html5
  rootfont edits under the lock: full comparator 361 to 362 ms (was 238 to
  250), update 344 to 356 ms (was 333 to 345). Sequential timing is
  unchanged by the fix (html5 rootfont edits 1 and 2: 793.6 and 796.7 ms
  before, 793.5 and 794.9 after). The par table of section 3 is the
  re-timing with the fixed driver; earlier in-process par timings of X5
  (steps 3 to 3c, runs/step3c.txt) used the same unordered readings.

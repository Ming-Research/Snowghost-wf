Snowghost’s layout stage builds and retains an owned tree of formatting contexts, prepares its text, computes geometry, and converts relative fragments into viewport rectangles. This description relies only on the Whitefoot source under `renderer/layout/`, principally `module.wfm`, `build.wf`, `prep.wf`, `box.wf`, `flow.wf`, and the update files.

Its construction inputs are `Document`, the style stage’s `Traversal` and computed `Styles`, an `AtomTable`, and a `Viewport`. Text preparation additionally takes a `FontSet` and matching `FontPicks`. `build_boxes` constructs boxes without shaping paragraphs; `prepare_text` prepares their text; `build_layout` combines those two operations. `lay_out` then computes geometry. The retained result is a `Layout`, containing the root `Context`, viewport, counts, edit lookup tables, dirty state, and update statistics. `place_boxes` produces `Slots<PlacedRect>`: each rectangle has an element/text `kind`, a DOM-node `owner`, and floating-point viewport coordinates. These are fragment rectangles corresponding to element or text client rectangles. Owners have no guaranteed overall ordering.

A `Context` represents a box establishing a formatting context. Its `kind` distinguishes flow, flex, grid, table, and replaced contexts. It owns `children`, plus local arrays of `Block`, `Paragraph`, `Piece`, `Flow`, fragments, and table-specific records. Consequently, most indices are **local to a context**, rather than global box identifiers. `Flow.Open` and `Flow.Close` index that context’s `blocks`; `Flow.Text` indexes its `paragraphs`; `Child`, `Float`, and `Out` index its `children`. `Block.parent` indexes another local block, or is `no_index`. `flow_at` records a position in the parent or owning context’s flow sequence. `TableCell.context` and table caption entries index child contexts; cell row and column values identify table-grid positions.

`StyleRef` provides a separate identity system. Its `element` is a `NodeId.index`, resolved through `Styles.order`; `root_element` is a special root-style reference. Its `pseudo`, when present, indexes the style stage’s pseudo-element list. An anonymous reference inherits inherited properties but uses initial non-inherited properties. `Fragment.owner`, `PlacedRect.owner`, and `Mark.Node.node` are DOM-node indices, not context indices. `no_index` and `no_pseudo` are sentinel values.

A `Paragraph.first` and `count` identify a span in its context’s `pieces`. `Piece.Bytes` identifies byte offsets in document text; `Generated` identifies byte offsets in style-generated text. Other pieces represent inserted scalars, inline boundaries, and atomic child contexts. Preparation produces Unicode scalar `text`, `TextRun`s, parallel `run_styles`, and `Mark`s. Run, mark, and line text offsets index this scalar array, not UTF-8 bytes. `TextRun.font` indexes `FontPicks.choices`; each choice selects a span in `FontPicks.chain`, whose entries index font-set faces. `ShapedText` stores scalar-indexed advances, break opportunities, and cluster flags, plus face segments.

Construction precedes preparation because `build_boxes` walks document content in tree order, constructs anonymous boxes, and resolves generated counters and quotes. `prepare_paragraph` decodes text, applies white-space processing and text transformation, builds marks and runs, calls `merge_runs`, and invokes `shape_paragraph`. Atomic inlines use U+FFFC; forced breaks use U+2028.

Geometry begins with `lay_out` assigning viewport space to the initial containing block. `lay_out_context` dispatches to the context-specific algorithm and records its completed `Space`. `Space` contains available width, percentage bases, optional forced dimensions, and shrink-to-fit state. Internal geometry uses signed integer units of 1/64 CSS pixel; `units`, `units_nearest`, and `units_up` implement distinct conversion rules.

For flow contexts, `lay_out_flow` runs in this order: resolve width; `prepare_spaces` to assign block widths, paragraph widths/left edges, and child spaces; lay out children; break paragraphs speculatively at full width; then `finish_flow`. That final function runs `stack_flow`, column processing, height/baseline resolution, positioned-child layout, and fragment generation. `stack_flow` preserves content order for collapsing margins, clearance, floats, and static positions; floats can force paragraphs to break again. Intrinsic widths are computed on demand through `intrinsic_sizes` and cached. Finally, `place_boxes` accumulates origins and applies column maps, splitting rectangles across columns when necessary.

Incremental text updates use `Layout.text_units`, indexed by DOM-node index. A `TextUnit` identifies a context serial, piece span, and paragraph. Context serials are preorder positions in `Layout.paths`; each `ContextPath` stores its parent’s serial and its index in that parent’s children. `text_changed`, `path_steps`, and `mark_path` locate and patch the affected pieces, then mark the paragraph and ancestors. Changes requiring different box construction are refused with `Inconsistent`.

`update` re-prepares marked paragraphs and calls `update_flow`. It keeps unmarked children when their space remains equivalent and preserves paragraph lines when their breaking inputs remain valid. `update_in_place` can avoid the pre-pass and stacking pass for constrained single-entry edits, translating later content by a height delta. `restack_flow` can resume stacking at the edited entry and stop when the remaining layout settles. `same_outputs` compares size, baseline, and margins for propagation cutoffs. Flex/grid `update_container` has an additional single-item cutoff involving intrinsic sizes. `styles_changed` validates retained construction dependencies before marking consumers; `structure_changed` rebuilds the nearest eligible context, reuses unchanged paragraph preparation, and checks counter/quote exit state before replacement.

Parallel opportunities follow these dependencies. `prepare_context` writes separate paragraphs and child contexts independently. After flow space assignment, `lay_out_children` can process children independently; paragraph breaking follows child layout because atomic metrics are inputs. Style-consumer marking, update loops, table intrinsic measurements, and several grid contribution/layout loops also write separate entries. Construction and `stack_flow` have ordered state dependencies; `reuse_prepared` explicitly remains sequential because swaps may alias old paragraphs. Actual worker scheduling or achieved runtime concurrency is **unknown** from this source.
